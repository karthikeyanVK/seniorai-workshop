# Enterprise Sales Master Playbook

## 1. How to use this playbook

This is the end-to-end Nexora sales method from first touch to renewal. Read it in order the first time; afterwards use the headings as a lookup. Each stage lists the goal, the actions, the evidence you must record in the CRM and the common failure. The short pages (Lead Qualification Guidelines, Discovery Framework, Negotiation Guidelines and others) are summaries of sections here; if they differ, escalate to Sales Enablement.

## 2. The sales motion at a glance

| Stage | Probability | Goal | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | 10% | Identify a target with a plausible need | Named account and contact |
| Qualification | 20% | Confirm need and budget | Budget owner and range recorded |
| Discovery | 40% | Map requirements and stakeholders | Decision maker and requirements recorded |
| Proposal | 60% | Deliver a priced proposal | Proposal and order form sent |
| Negotiation | 80% | Agree commercial and legal terms | Redlines, discount approval, procurement owner |
| Closed Won / Lost | 100% / 0% | Sign or learn | Signature or loss reason and competitor |

Typical cycle length: Starter 15 to 60 days, Professional 30 to 110 days, Enterprise 70 to 180 days. A deal far outside its band needs a manager conversation.

## 3. Stage 1: Prospecting and lead qualification

### 3.1 Goal

Turn interest into a conversation with someone who can buy. Lead score reflects interest, not qualification. A lead scoring 90 because of content downloads is not qualified until a two-way conversation has taken place.

### 3.2 The five criteria

Qualify every lead on Budget (a named owner and range), Authority (a decision maker or confirmed sponsor), Need (a pain tied to forecasting, pipeline visibility or renewals), Timeline (a decision inside 180 days) and Fit (at least 50 employees and a CRM in place).

### 3.3 Speed rules

- Score of 80 or more: first touch within 24 hours.
- Other leads: first touch within 3 business days.
- Score of 85 or more with no touch in 14 days: the regional manager reassigns it.
- Three unanswered attempts: move to nurture, not pipeline.

### 3.4 Disqualification codes

No Budget, No Need, Competitor Locked-in, Student or Research, Duplicate. Always record the code; the monthly review depends on it.

### 3.5 Common failures

Letting a lead sit with a rep on leave; ignoring a lead because the region on the record does not match the company headquarters; treating download volume as intent. Fix the first by reassigning within two business days and the second by checking headquarters before routing.

## 4. Stage 2: Discovery

### 4.1 The three-call structure

**Call 1, Situation.** Current tools, team size, how the forecast is built, accuracy last quarter. **Call 2, Impact.** Quantify the cost of slipped deals, stale pipeline and surprise churn. **Call 3, Vision.** Agree success criteria and who signs.

### 4.2 Questions that must be asked

- Who else is involved in the decision?
- What happens if you do nothing?
- What have you tried already?
- Which integrations are mandatory: CRM, ERP, billing?
- Where must data be hosted, and why?
- Does the company need SSO, audit logs or a security review?

### 4.3 The residency and security rule

Capture data residency, SSO and security review requirements in the first two calls. Late discovery of these is the most common reason deals stall in Negotiation or are lost to a competitor with an existing regional footprint. If the buyer needs EU hosting, the plan is Enterprise. If the buyer needs APAC or India hosting, flag it to Product immediately and do not promise a date.

### 4.4 Evidence to record

Decision maker, economic buyer, champion, competitor, requirements list, timeline and the next customer-side action with a date.

## 5. Stage 3: Proposal

### 5.1 Build the proposal around impact

Lead with the quantified problem from discovery, then the plan and modules that address it, then price. Send the order form with the proposal, not afterwards.

### 5.2 Choose the right plan

Do not discount Enterprise to win a buyer who only needs Starter or Professional features. Sell the right plan. Check what the plan already includes before adding modules; SAML SSO is already in Professional and Enterprise.

### 5.3 Legal and security in parallel

For Enterprise deals (ACV of $100,000 or more), start the legal and security reviews at Proposal. Security questionnaires owned by procurement with no named owner stall for weeks.

### 5.4 Confirm the economic buyer

By Proposal stage the economic buyer and signing authority must be confirmed. A champion is not an economic buyer. Record both.

## 6. Stage 4: Negotiation

### 6.1 Principles

Anchor on value before price. Quantify forecast accuracy gains and avoided churn first. Pre-agree your walk-away: floor discount, minimum term and payment terms (net 45 maximum for Enterprise).

### 6.2 Concession order

1. Onboarding services.
2. Extended term.
3. Price lock for year two.
4. Discount within policy.

Trade, do not give: every concession is exchanged for something such as a multi-year term, a reference or a faster signature.

### 6.3 Discount authority

| Discount | Approver |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10% | Sales rep (Starter capped at 10%) |
| Above 10% to 20% | Regional Sales Manager |
| Above 20% to 30% | VP Sales and Finance |
| Above 30% | Chief Revenue Officer, written exception, multi-year or strategic logo only |

Approval must be recorded in the CRM before the order form is sent. Renewals may not be discounted more than 5% without regional manager approval. A discount above 30% with no recorded CRO exception is a policy breach.

### 6.4 The 45-day rule

A deal in Negotiation for more than 45 days needs a manager-led call within 5 business days. Identify the blocker: legal, pricing or procurement. Each needs a different action plan.

### 6.5 Common blockers

- **Legal:** redlines on the DPA liability cap. Remedy: involve Legal early with a pre-approved fallback.
- **Pricing:** a request for a further discount after authority is used. Remedy: trade for a 24-month term or a price lock, and escalate.
- **Procurement:** vendor onboarding with no named owner. Remedy: ask the buyer to name one and send the vendor pack immediately.

## 7. Competitive handling

| Competitor | Where it appears | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Vantara Revenue Cloud | Europe Mid-Market, since March 2026 | Compare three-year TCO including partner-led implementation; be honest about its longer EU hosting history |
| PipelineIQ | Mid-Market buyers who only need visibility | Reporting depth, contract and renewal workflows |
| ClearPath CRM | SMB, North America and India | Starter plan and migration help |
| Strata Sales Suite | Australia, Singapore, Japan | Data Connect, global reporting and local partners |
| Orbit Revenue | Technology sector | Proven scale, security pack, support SLAs and audited AI answers |

Log the competitor on every opportunity. Loss reasons feed the monthly win/loss review.

## 8. Objection handling

### 8.1 Pricing objections

1. **A competitor is 20 to 30 percent cheaper.** Re-anchor on total cost and modules included.
2. **Budget is frozen.** Offer quarterly billing or a phased start.
3. **We only need a few features.** Sell the right plan rather than discounting Enterprise.
4. **We want a multi-year price lock.** Trade the lock for a 24-month term.

### 8.2 Product objections

- **Where can data be hosted?** Frankfurt on Enterprise; APAC planned for Q1 2027; no India region. Never imply otherwise.
- **Does it have SSO?** Yes on Professional and Enterprise.
- **Can it sync with our CRM?** Yes with Data Connect.
- **Is it fast on large data?** State the known issue NX-4412 and its targeted fix date, 2026-12-02.

### 8.3 How to log objections

Write the objection and the response in the activity notes. The same objection unresolved after two calls triggers a manager call and reduces the opportunity priority score by 20 percent.

## 9. Multi-threading and stakeholders

At least three contacts including a decision maker on every Enterprise deal. If the champion goes quiet, contact the economic buyer within 5 days with a short value recap. Record the finance owner on renewals, because overdue renewal invoices are usually a purchase order or contact change problem and rarely a real dispute.

## 10. Closing

A close is a mutual plan with dated milestones: security review, legal redlines, procurement onboarding, signature. Confirm the economic buyer by Proposal stage. If there is no customer-side activity for 21 days, re-engage the champion and economic buyer separately. Quarter-end urgency works only when the customer has a real reason; invented deadlines increase discount requests.

## 11. Stalled deals

A deal with no activity for 30 days is stalled. Steps: check the last customer-side response, send a break-up style email offering a decision date, offer a manager call and, if there is no reply in 14 days, close as Lost with the reason No Decision. Stalled deals left in Proposal or Negotiation inflate the forecast.

## 12. Prioritisation

Rank open opportunities weekly by expected value, which is deal value multiplied by stage probability. Adjust: plus 20 percent if a decision maker was met in the last 14 days, minus 30 percent if there has been no activity in 21 days, minus 20 percent if the same objection stands after two calls. No rep may carry more than 25 open opportunities (15 for Enterprise reps). Deals whose expected close date has passed are reviewed first: re-date with a reason or close.

## 13. Renewal and expansion

### 13.1 Cadence

Start 120 days before renewal_date. At 120 days run a health review, at 90 days a value review with the sponsor, at 60 days send the renewal quote and at 30 days complete paperwork.

### 13.2 Escalation triggers

Escalate to the regional manager and involve Customer Success when churn probability is 0.45 or higher, or usage has fallen two snapshots in a row.

### 13.3 Expansion signals

Usage above 90 percent, plan limits reached, or requests for features in a higher plan. Cross-sell in this order of preference: Data Connect, Revenue Analytics, Conversation Intelligence, Security and Compliance Pack.

## 14. Self-check before moving a deal forward

- Is the decision maker named and have I spoken with them?
- Is there a dated next step on the customer side?
- Is the competitor recorded?
- Have residency, SSO and security requirements been captured?
- Is the discount approved and recorded in the CRM?
- Has the expected close date been re-dated with a reason, if it moved?
